# Skill · Content & voice

The copy carries as much of the product's credibility as the visuals. Tutors are
professionals under time pressure; every line either helps them act or gets cut.

## Voice: plain and factual

State what is true, then what to do. No enthusiasm, no hedging, no personality.

```
✗  "Great news! Bella is making awesome progress! 🎉"
✓  "Bella's mastery rose 4% after this worksheet."

✗  "It seems like there might be some issues with keywords."
✓  "Nine of 14 students omitted the required keyword in Q3."
```

Never exclamation marks. Never emoji. Never "AI-powered", "leverage", "insights at your
fingertips", "seamlessly", "empower".

## AI copy has three obligations

Every AI statement must give the finding, the evidence, and the stake:

```
finding    "60% of students struggle connecting structural adaptations to survival advantage."
evidence   "Appears in 4 of the last 5 assessments."
stake      "Worth 6–8 marks in Booklet B every paper."
```

AI feedback to students names the gap and the fix, in the mark scheme's language:

> "You correctly identified that the fat keeps the fox warm. To gain full marks, state that
> fat is a poor conductor of heat and that this reduces heat loss from the body to the
> surroundings."

AI reasoning defends the score in marking terms:

> "One of three model elements present. The answer states the effect but not the mechanism,
> which the mark scheme requires for the second mark."

## Curriculum vocabulary (Singapore PSLE)

Use the real terms — they are what tutors search, say, and bill for:

```
OEQ · MCQ · Structured          question types
Booklet A / Booklet B           paper sections
Primary 4 / 5 / 6, PSLE Prep    levels
mark scheme · marking points    the standard
keyword · phrasing · technique  the common failure modes
mastery · weak topic            the product's own measures
```

Content topics come from the syllabus: Cycles in Matter & Water, Energy Forms & Uses, Living
Things & Environment, Plant Transport System, Interactions & Adaptation; Fractions,
Ratio & Proportion, Area/Perimeter/Volume, Decimals & Percentage, Rate & Speed.

Keep one subject world per demo. Mixing P5 Science with Calculus and Creative Writing
signals nobody thought about the user.

## Labels

```
buttons     sentence case, explicit object — "Generate Worksheet", "Approve & Next Question"
eyebrows    UPPERCASE with .13em tracking — QUICK ACTIONS, AI INSIGHT, SUBMISSION DETAILS
columns     UPPERCASE with .09em tracking — STUDENT, STATUS, WEAK TOPIC
badges      UPPERCASE with .05em tracking — NEEDS PRACTICE, FOCUS AREA
titles      sentence case, phrased as the tutor's task — "Who is this worksheet for?"
```

## Numbers

```
mastery, topic %   whole numbers, no decimals
marks, scores      one decimal (1.0 / 2.0) — half marks exist
submissions        fractions, not percentages — "12/14 submitted"
deltas             always signed — +12%, −3%
counts             numerals, spelled out only mid-sentence ("Six students may need…")
```

Tabular numerals (```font-variant-numeric:tabular-nums```) in any column of figures.

## Names and data

Demo content must look like a real Singapore tuition centre roster: Bella Tan, Jayden Lim,
Chloe Tan, Marcus Koh, Nadia Rahman, Ethan Wong, Ryan Ng, Sarah Lee. Realistic classes
("Primary 5 Science", Mon 4:00 PM, 15 students), realistic dates in one term.

Student answers must read like a real 11-year-old's writing, mistakes intact:

> "The hotter the water the faster it evaporate."

That imperfection is what makes the marking flow legible — a grammatically perfect answer
gives the AI nothing to find.

## Rules

- Never lorem ipsum, never "Lorem", never "Sample text", never "Question text here".
- Never invent statistics that contradict visible data — copy and numbers come from one source.
- Never claim the AI is certain. It suggests; the tutor decides.
- Sentence-per-line prose; no paragraph longer than three sentences in UI copy.
